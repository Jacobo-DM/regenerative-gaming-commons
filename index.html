02 · Boundary

This is not gamification.

Regenerative gaming begins by refusing the extractive habits that have been smuggled into play: coercion, scoring, compulsion loops, surveillance, and the conversion of attention into profit.

This is not gamification. Not behavioral extraction dressed as care. Not a productivity system. Not a product. Not a dashboard for measuring aliveness.

No capture

A game should not harvest attention, biometric data, emotional vulnerability, or community trust.

No false urgency

The system should not create panic, streak anxiety, scarcity loops, or artificial fear of missing out.

No hollow care

The language of healing should not be used to disguise extraction, monetization, surveillance, or institutional capture.

04 · Principles

A game can be a shelter for attention.

Regenerative games do not begin with points. They begin with conditions: safety, curiosity, consent, embodied presence, story, and the possibility of repair.

Restore attention

The game should leave the player more able to notice, feel, listen, choose, and return.

Protect small beings

Design for children, overwhelmed nervous systems, elders, tired teachers, and fragile rooms.

Keep the body present

Breath, gesture, rhythm, pause, balance, and place are not decorations. They are part of the play.

Let story carry the ethic

A story envelope helps the practice remain human, memorable, and harder to flatten into technique.

Make repair possible

The game should allow stopping, re-entering, changing roles, apologizing, laughing, and beginning again.

Stay adaptable

The seed should work with what is already present: a room, a circle, a stone, a voice, a hand, a patch of ground.

A regenerative game does not ask, “How do we keep the player engaged?”

It asks, “What kind of attention does this game make possible — and what happens to the player when the game is over?”
